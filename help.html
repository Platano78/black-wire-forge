How Black Wire Forge works

Black Wire Forge makes pictures, video, sound and 3D on your own machines. Pick the job first, the engine second. Everything you make lands in History, and rendering stays on your own configured machines. The one exception: if the optional prompt helper is set up, "Help me write this" and "Describe this picture" send your text (and, for a picture, the image) to that helper's own endpoint — see the FAQ below.

The screen

  • Top bar: one block per machine, showing its state and how busy it is. Click one to make it the machine your next job goes to.
  • Rooms (the row under the top bar): one room per kind of job, grouped by Sound, Picture, Motion and Object. The Cutting Room at the right end is where the things you make go together.
  • History (left): everything made in this room, or everything, with the switch at the top.
  • Monitor (centre): the thing you picked in History, or the room's guidance when nothing is picked.
  • Inspector (right): the room's engines, then the recipe, your words or files, quality, and Make.
  • Activity (bottom): what the app has been doing, line by line.

Machines

Each machine shows one of six states, each with its own word and dot shape, so you never have to read a colour. WORKING has a second label, QUEUED, for the same state (rendering/queued jobs share a dot shape; QUEUED means one of yours is in line but nothing is rendering there yet):

statemeans
READYanswering, and has what it needs
WORKING (or QUEUED)rendering now, or a job of yours is queued there (the count shows how far)
ASLEEPnot answering, nothing waiting for it
ASLEEP, WORK WAITINGnot answering, and a job of yours is waiting for it
CANNOT TELLstopped answering mid-way; the app will not guess
UP, NOT FOR THISanswering, but it has none of the models this app uses

Some engines run on a different kind of machine: for example, turning a 3D model around runs as a program on the app's own machine, on its processor. Its row says runs on <machine>; pick it and the app switches machine for you.

Making something

  1. Pick a room, then an engine in the inspector. Each engine says in one line when to pick it, and carries its licence stamp.
  2. Pick a recipe. A recipe fills in settings that were measured to work, and its note (in italics) says why.
  3. Write, or add files. Pictures and sound go to the machine that will use them. A 3D model is a .glb file.
  4. Pick a quality. Named stops, not raw numbers. An estimate like "about 40 s" appears once the same engine and quality has finished three times on that machine; until then it shows -- rather than a guess.
  5. Everything else (N) opens every other setting. You can change any number. Your change wins; picking a recipe or a quality afterwards rewrites the numbers that recipe or quality sets.
  6. Make. The job appears in History straight away as a ghost with its progress, and is replaced by the result when it finishes.

The room's guide

Every room has a guide: a helper that knows that room's engines. It sits in the inspector, right under the prompt box and its "Help me write this" button. It is not a separate app: it talks through the optional prompt helper set up in config.json.

  • Help me write this: tell it in a few words what you want (in the prompt box), press the button, and the guide writes the fields for that mode. It may ask you one question at a time first; when a question has choices, click one or type your own answer. It asks at most 4 questions, then writes anyway, using sensible defaults and naming each default it picked.
  • The preview: what it wrote appears in the guide panel field by field. Nothing reaches your form until you press Use these; Dismiss throws it away and Start over begins again. It never starts a job: you still press Make.
  • Talking to it: you can also just ask the guide a question in its box (Enter sends, Shift+Enter makes a new line). The conversation stays in your browser.
  • Compact / Verbose: how much the guide knows. Compact suits small models and is the default; Verbose knows more but needs a model with more context. The panel says how much context each needs, and warns you if your helper reports less, but it never switches for you.
  • Describe this picture: when the mode has a picture in it (one you added, or a result you picked in History), the guide can write the prompt from the picture. The picture is only sent to a helper that can see pictures.
  • Not right? Tell the guide: on a finished result, press it, say what is wrong, and the guide says what went wrong and gives a revised prompt (or an edit to try). A helper that cannot see pictures says so and goes by your words.
  • Edit this result: on a finished picture, opens the room's edit mode with that picture already in place as picture 1; say what to change, then press Make.
  • No helper set up? The guide panel still shows the room's plain guidance, and says how to add a helper. "Help me write this" only appears once a helper is set up.

Your first five minutes

Every room with something it can make has its own Try this button: one click fills in a real recipe and settings for you, so you can see what a finished result looks like before you write a word yourself. Nothing is made until you press Make. Three to try first:

  1. Make a picture from words, in the Picture room.
  2. Make a song, in the Music room.
  3. Turn your picture into a 3D model, in the 3D room — turning that model around afterwards needs a 3D model first, so make one here before you try it.

History

  • This room / Everything switches between this room's things and everything you have made. Picking something from another room takes you to that room.
  • Use again puts that job's settings back in the inspector.
  • Stop ends a job that is still going.
  • Forget removes the record from History. The file itself is never deleted by this app.

Sharing and licences

Every result carries a licence stamp. These are informational badges about the engine's own licence metadata, not a legal clearance — review the model licence and the rights in your prompts, references and output before publishing anything you make here:

  • yours to use (green): the engine's licence carries no recorded distribution gate.
  • conditions apply: read the licence under Credits before you publish.
  • non-commercial (red): only when the licence itself says NC.

Download (recipe removed) strips the settings and words hidden inside a picture, audio or video file before it leaves the app — for the formats it can actually clean right now (JPEG/WebP are re-encoded, not byte-for-byte untouched; not every audio format is supported, e.g. WAV/M4A are not). If a file can't actually be cleaned, the download is refused with a plain sentence rather than quietly handing you the original under that label — use "Keep the recipe" if you want the original regardless. 3D model files download exactly as made. Keep the recipe downloads the file with everything inside it. Credits lists every engine and its licence.

The Cutting Room

This is where pictures, shots and sound are put in order and cut together into one film. A sequence is one film. Its timeline has a lane each for pictures, video shots and sound, plus the REF ROOM for the pictures that keep the place and the people the same from shot to shot. Your first film, step by step:

  1. Start a sequence. Press New sequence. Leave the title empty and it is named from the date, then from its first beat. Click its name any time to rename it. Each row in the list shows the first beat, how many shots it has, a small picture and when it was last changed, with Rename and Delete.
  2. Tell the Film Room Guide the idea. One line is enough. It asks what it needs and suggests the beats. What it suggests comes as buttons under its reply: Add these beats to the script, Open or Make a shot, put a picture in the REF ROOM, Cut the sequence. Nothing happens until you click one. Adding the beats gives each one its own video shot, with the beat's words already in its prompt.
  3. Write each shot. Click a shot in the timeline to open it. Write this shot has the guide turn its beat into a prompt written for that shot's engine. Nothing reaches the form until you press Use these.
  4. Start a shot from a picture (optional). Make a picture on the PICTURE lane with + generate here. It already comes out in the film's shape (16:9 unless you changed it). Then either press Use as starting picture on it, or open a video shot and use Choose a picture you made under its Starting picture: that lists this film's pictures, the REF ROOM and History. You can also patch a cable: click the small circle on a video shot's left edge, then the picture. A picture of the wrong shape is pointed out before anything renders.
  5. Keep the place the same. Put a picture of the empty location in the REF ROOM as the set plate. The note under a video shot says whether that shot can use the REF ROOM, and offers the fix as a button.
  6. Make, then pick. Press Make on each shot. When a take is ready, check it at full size in the monitor and press Pick this take. Make again for another take; every take is kept. A shot you opened and never touched is not kept, and an empty shot has a × to remove it.
  7. Add sound. + generate here on the SOUND lane starts at the length of the cut so far. Pick its take and it becomes the music bed under the whole film.
  8. Cut it. Cut the sequence joins every picked video shot in timeline order, with the music bed under them and one loudness pass over the whole film. Each video shot's Cut settings trim its length and add a title card. Watch the cut plays it in the monitor, and Download saves the file. Cutting needs ffmpeg/ffprobe installed.

Prefer to paste a whole script? Switch the sequence to storyboard: each paragraph becomes a beat with its own shot.

Setting up

This section is for whoever runs the app, not whoever uses it day to day.

  • config.json lives next to server.py (or wherever GENCENTER_CONFIG points). It is not in the repo -- start from config.example.json. There must be at least one lane; an empty "lanes" list is refused at startup, with a message saying so.
  • A lane is usually one machine running its own ComfyUI: an id, a name shown in the UI, and host/port for its ComfyUI API. caps -- which kinds of job it's for (image, video, audio, 3d) -- is optional here: leave it out and the lane is considered for everything it can actually be shown to run. A process lane instead runs a local program on the app's own machine (no ComfyUI, no host/port) and must declare caps. To add a lane, add another entry to the "lanes" list and restart the app; nothing else needs to change.
  • allowed_hosts lists any extra name (besides localhost and the machine's own IP/hostname) the app should answer requests addressed to -- an mDNS name or a reverse proxy, for example. A name not on this list is refused, even from inside your LAN.
  • "CANNOT TELL" means a machine stopped answering mid-way through checking on it -- not that it's asleep, and not that a job it was running failed. The app will not guess which; see the Machines section above.
  • Logs aren't written to a file: the app prints one line per event to its own stdout (wherever you started python3 server.py from, or redirected it to), and keeps the most recent 250 of those lines in memory for the Activity panel at the bottom of the screen. Restarting the app clears that in-memory copy.

FAQ

Why is a room dimmed?
Nothing installed can make that kind of thing yet. It stays in the list so you can see what the tool is for.
Why does an engine say "needs …"?
The active machine is missing that model or program; the words name it. Pick another machine above, or install it.
Why does a machine say CANNOT TELL instead of idle?
It stopped answering. The app will not pretend it is idle, or that your job is still rendering.
A job says lost.
The app restarted while a program was running on its own machine, or a job has been waiting more than six hours. Make it again.
Where are my files?
On the machine that made them. Things made on the app's own machine are kept in the app's data folder. Download copies a file to you.
Why is there no time estimate?
It needs three finished jobs of the same engine and quality on that machine. It never borrows a number from somewhere else.
Does it need the internet?
No. The page itself loads no fonts or scripts from anywhere. Rendering stays on your own configured machines. The one exception is the optional prompt helper (see below) — if it is set up, whatever you send it goes wherever that helper's own endpoint is.
Can I run two things at once?
Each machine takes its own queue. A process lane (the app's own machine, or another one running a local program) runs up to its configured worker count at once — one at a time by default.
What does "Help me write this" do?
It hands the words in the prompt box, or a picture you have, to the room's guide, which writes a prompt in the style this mode expects. The answer appears in the guide panel right below the button. Only offered when the app was set up with a helper; you always see what it wrote first and choose whether to use it. It never starts a job on its own. Your text — and, for "Describe this picture", the image itself when the helper can see pictures — is sent to that helper's own endpoint; its operator and privacy policy apply, not this app's.
